# Research fit and next evidence

**Review date: 15 October 2026 (Australia/Sydney).** The strongest current contribution is a small, inspectable experiment about application-enforced tool authority. Its value depends on correct measurement and reproducibility, not the size of the research archive or the number of tests.

General fellowship-selection guidance emphasizes concrete technical work, research relevance, collaboration, and external scholarly signals. That guidance is not an individualized explanation of an application outcome, and it is not OpenAI's grant rubric. Nothing here predicts selection, attributes an evaluator's private reasoning, or supplies the owner's career intentions.

## Program fit

The [current OpenAI Cybersecurity Grant form](https://openai.com/form/cybersecurity-grant-program/) requests a focused defensive idea, evidence about gaps, a method, models, datasets, public sharing, timeline, and justified resources. The [February 2026 program update](https://openai.com/index/trusted-access-for-cyber/) emphasizes operational cyber defense and teams with demonstrated vulnerability-remediation experience. Simurgh offers a defensive measurement instrument and an inspectable implementation. The present synthetic demonstration does not establish deployed security impact, an external vulnerability-remediation record, applicant eligibility, or likely funding.

The fit is strongest when the proposal asks a narrow question: **can a boundary prevent unauthorized effects while preserving legitimate work, and can a reviewer distinguish trustworthy evidence from a misleading success label?** No current result establishes that an OpenAI model is susceptible to the authored attacks, that the boundary protects a production integration, or that an external organization has reproduced it.

## Next experiment: held-out authority and consequence tests

This is a proposed follow-up, not a completed study or funded commitment.

1. **Freeze the present instrument.** Record its source hashes, fixture manifest, policy, scoring rules, and known limitations. Distinguish development fixtures from held-out material. Keep the existing denominator intact.
2. **Define transfer before seeing outcomes.** Ask a separate contributor to author cases in two declared families: resource/approval substitution and attempted information disclosure. Include matched legitimate tasks and unrelated service failures. Keep final cases hidden from the implementer until the boundary and scorer are frozen. If no separate contributor is available, label the extension internally authored and do not claim independence.
3. **Use two separate estimands.** Offline runs measure boundary/oracle behavior for supplied proposals. An explicitly authorized Responses API arm measures model-generated proposals and end-to-end outcomes. Reuse each obtained proposal across guarded and control executions to separate model behavior from enforcement effects. Do not pool these results into a single safety percentage.
4. **Predeclare accounting.** Report every attempted case, policy violation, legitimate completion, refusal, malformed call, provider failure, and stopping event. Report raw counts before rates. For repeated live trials, define the unit of analysis and dependence assumptions before attaching intervals. One deterministic authored case is not an independent population sample.
5. **Check external reproduction and disagreement.** Have another operator install the pinned environment and run the exported task and verifier. Compare canonical payloads, scorer outcomes, and failure behavior; record discrepancies as findings. A fresh local process or a self-generated key is not an independent witness.
6. **Publish a bounded result after review.** Share source, synthetic fixtures, methodology, actual results, errors, and the next research question. Record what was not tested: multi-step agents, browser/network effects, real credentials, production connectors, informed human approvals, and compromised host/oracle behavior. Publication and paid API use need separate owner authorization.

**Proposed models:** `gpt-6.1-sol` as a primary general-purpose model and `gpt-6-luna` as a lower-cost comparison, based on the [official catalog](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models) retrieved on the review date. These are candidate choices, not an availability check against the applicant's account or evidence from a live run. Confirm account access, endpoint support, a dated snapshot if offered, token budget, and the returned model identifier before starting. A model's provider description is not a security ranking established by this project.

The next credible improvement is an externally challenged, held-out result with useful-work preservation and explicit failures. More promotional wording, simulated endorsements, or relabeling synthetic counts as model performance would not supply that evidence.
