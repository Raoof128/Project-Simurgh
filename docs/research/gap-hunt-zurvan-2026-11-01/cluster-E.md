## Trend across the four updates

Short answer: not agentic misuse in OpenAI's own words. The word "agentic" appears in none of the four updates, nor in the distillation or false-front pages. No page describes a model acting with delegated authority on a victim's systems. What grows is automation around the API and use of several models in one workflow.

Reading with scepticism: the four reports are structurally blind to agentic misuse. They see prompts sent to OpenAI, and say so ("we are not able to independently verify any off-platform activity", row 9). An actor who runs an agent loop on their own infrastructure or on another vendor's model leaves little for OpenAI to see. Absence of agentic cases here is weak evidence about the world. The cluster's strongest agent-adjacent evidence is not agents at all. It is replay of a protected artifact across conversations, and compaction attack paths (rows 1 to 3).

## Top 5 for this cluster (ranked by how directly an authorization/verification runtime pays it)

1. Row 1: encrypted reasoning replayable by whoever held it. This is the audience-binding failure in G14 and N06 and the only item with a lab-admitted real fix. A cross-binding replay lane pays it.
2. Row 3: compaction and cross-model attack paths confirmed real by OpenAI. This is the lab-confirmed instance of N04 and G26 that Simurgh has not exercised.
3. Rows 7 to 9: harm in composition outside the issuing runtime, a detection unit that is cross-request, and a lab that cannot verify off-platform effects. This is the strongest outside statement of the receiver-evidence thesis, and a composition corpus pays it.
4. Rows 11 and 12: revocation that is not durable. Accounts are rotated per conversation and actors return after bans. A re-entry and rotation lane pays N02 and G14.
5. Row 4: tool-output attacks that visible-text checks miss, with partner-hosted deployments unprotected. A hidden-channel tool response lane pays N08.
