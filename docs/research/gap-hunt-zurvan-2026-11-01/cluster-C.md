## Top 5 for this cluster (ranked by how directly an authorization/verification runtime pays it)

1. **Access-and-redaction ledger for evaluators (rows 1, 2, 3, 8).** The lab says there are no standards for access or reporting, keeps the redaction decision, and pays one evaluator directly. A signed manifest of what was shown, withheld and committed by hash, with a verifier, is exactly the omission-and-custody artifact G18, G19 and N10 ask for.
2. **Receiver-side coverage statistic (row 11).** The lab defines oversight as the share of actions passing through a monitor, computed by itself, and writes that outsiders will need measurements that are "complete, accurate, and technically enforced". Coverage recomputed from receiver snapshots by a third party is buildable from the existing archive.
3. **Forged context provenance lane (row 24).** A real attack moved protected blocks between conversations to make the model believe access was already granted, and the lab's fix only covers accounts created after 31 Aug 2026. Nothing in the registers tests authority surviving a transplanted block (N04 is open).
4. **Intended-use and target-scope manifest (rows 4, 5).** Both verification programs bind access to a prose or asserted scope and enforce it by classifier or offline monitoring. A machine-readable manifest enforced pre-effect, with an offline reconciliation, is the Simurgh composition applied to a published program.
5. **Approval independence record (row 13).** No register item asks whether an approval reflects the approver's own judgment. Commit-then-reveal capture plus a deferral rate is cheap to build and measures the control most designs rely on.

Runners-up with direct fit: rows 22 and 23 (advertised-versus-embedded type and fail-open on unresolvable identifiers) strengthen G01, G02 and G08 with in-the-wild evidence; row 21 is the unregistered G28 candidate.

## Stated acceptance criteria (verification and evaluator programs)

What an outside party must show, in the programs' own terms, and what is not stated.

- **Independent evaluators (15 Oct).** Four needs: expertise, independence (no equity, not financially or operationally dependent), enough access (models, training visibility, safeguards), freedom to publish. Three access tiers; tier one publishes with Anthropic's approval, tier two without it but with redaction, tier three is employee-like access (METR, six staff). Not stated: how independence or access is evidenced, or how redactions are audited.
- **Accenture (18 Sep).** Both parties expect to invest at least $1 billion each over five years. Anthropic funds Accenture directly. Not stated: any standard for access or reporting (row 2).
