## Memory notes

- **Figures.** The Argon report has 86 untranscribed figures. All prompt-injection survival numbers (Fig 6.2-6.9, PDF pages 204-211), agent behaviour numbers (Fig 5.5-5.9) and monitor-evasion charts are invisible. The text gives no survival percentage anywhere. Do not anchor claims to them; the report's own exec summary says only that the model "resisted attacks in most test scenarios". Figure companion notes would be needed first.
- **Duplicates.** Model Card (web, 13 Oct 2026) and Model Card-2 (PDF) carry the same safety text; the PDF loses the benchmark table to an image. Stealth paper -2 is the full IASEAI paper, the other is an abstract stub. Backdoors -2 is the full paper, the other an abstract stub. Nothing to re-read in the non-numbered copies.
- **Version drift.** The Stealth paper tested 2025 models with 5 stealth challenges and thresholds 9/11 and 4/5. ### Direct answers to the lead's five asks

- **(a) Prompt-injection defences.** The report claims "significantly increasing the expected cost for an adaptive attacker to find a working exploit" and that Argon "successfully resisted attacks in most test scenarios". Caveats it states itself: static benchmarks "saturate quickly"; the attacker is Gemini 3.8 Flash unless noted; survival is measured at N attempts (15 for contextual, 40 for RAG); computer-use ran without the out-of-model detector. No survival percentage is in the text, so no number can be quoted; all are in untranscribed figures. It does not say the defence is complete. Rows 1-4.
- **(b) Whose job is agent authority?** Never stated. Agent-misuse evals test "the model alone", with "additional safeguards ... where appropriate". The loss-of-control scope excludes scaffolding errors and improper deployment. The lab itself relies on "monitoring – alongside sandboxing and access controls" and concedes sandboxes are not "impervious". Rows 5, 6.
- **(c) Evaluation awareness and monitoring.** Thresholds: stealth pass = beat top 50% of humans; situational awareness pass = over 20% success; inability case valid below 9/11 and 4/5. Awareness: 0.0% to 37.8% unprompted in honeypots; 75.8-94.0% on stealth challenges. The lab says monitoring evidence is conditional on five assumptions it has not fully verified, and refusals caused by awareness are "observationally indistinguishable" from sandbagging. It anchors the loss-of-control case on runtime monitoring and control, not on alignment benchmarks. Rows 10-16.
- **(d) Backdoor paper.** Threat model trusts batching, routing and compiler; adversary may be supply chain, insider or the hosting provider. Verification offered: static taint proof on the ONNX graph, 361 of 1680 proven safe, valid only for the checked shapes, dynamic models out of reach, otherwise "prioritize models from verified, trusted sources". An outside party can rerun the open-source checker on a graph; it cannot verify the running server. Rows 24-26.
- **(e) Pre-deployment requirements with no externally checkable artifact.** (1) The governance-function determination of acceptable residual risk, which is internal (row 20). (2) The safety case required where a CCL is reached; the report says one was "developed" but reproduces none (row 20). (3) The FSF says specific mitigations "may be determined when a T/CCL is reached", so they are not fixed in advance. (4) The control-evaluation requirement in FSF 2.0 has disappeared from FSF 3.1 and the Argon report (row 21). (5) Independent evaluators do not assess the thresholds (row 19). (6) Disclosure to governments is "we aim to share". (7) Weight security is self-declared "SL2+".
